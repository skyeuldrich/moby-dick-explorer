# Issue 14 — species motion and organic encounter

The three owner-approved transparent paintings replace the earlier five SVG motifs in both dark-water scenes. The original PNGs extracted from `moby-dick-deep-sea-creatures.zip` are retained unchanged. Each has an alpha channel spanning 0–255, transparent corners, and the specified native dimensions. Lossless WebP derivatives reduce transfer size without changing the painted pixels or alpha. These are modern artistic reconstructions, **not archival illustrations or verified species portraits**. The whale/squid painting remains unchanged.

## Motion and visibility

Scroll sets world placement and fading; a separate clock animates anatomy while the visitor is stationary. A small 12×8 image mesh bends the original surfaces: occasional angler fin corrections and a separately displaced lure; a short jelly bell contraction and longer relaxation with delayed trailing structures; sparse loosejaw tail waves between predatory pauses. Jelly propulsion follows the supplied image's right-facing bell axis. Light organs use the same deformation coordinates as their body landmarks, including the loosejaw's small red facial light and individual blue-green underside points.

The renderer prepares 36 small frames per species, yields during preparation, interpolates adjacent frames at approximately 30 updates/second, and stops drawing hidden scenes or a hidden tab. Reduced motion uses still frames and removes camera travel. Bodies remain much dimmer than tiny light organs and fade with them before the desk dominates. The masking encounter is scroll-only: separate broad soft fields reveal head, arms, then torso over the last 13% of the narrative. Additive mask composition preserves independent early contours; no hard wipe or rectangular background is added.
