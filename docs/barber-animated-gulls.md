# Barber’s 1839 Nantucket engraving and its wheeling gulls

The founder selected the engraving by requesting that its own birds move with scrolling. The harbor now displays John Warner Barber’s *South-Eastern View of Nantucket, Mass.* (1839), engraved by S. E. Brown, Boston. This replaces the original cartoon harbor. The image is identified as an **animated archival adaptation**, not an untouched nineteenth-century moving image.

Source: [Internet Archive book and page](https://archive.org/details/historicalcollec00barb_0/page/n480/mode/1up), [original JPEG](https://archive.org/download/historicalcollec00barb_0/page/n480.jpg). The US1839 publication is public domain through copyright expiration; [Cornell’s term table](https://copyright.cornell.edu/publicdomain). Source dimensions, bytes, and SHA-256 are recorded in the archival manifest and verified by the asset integrity script.

`public/assets/archive/barber-1839-source.jpg` holds the downloaded page. The harbor displays a grayscale crop of its sky, waterfront and ships. Three small PNGs isolate the original ink strokes of the birds against transparent pixels, including paper visible through openings in their wings. Three stationary alpha patches restore the paper immediately beneath the original ink. This modern adaptation preserves access to the full scan in Field notes.

The bird layers travel on different shallow arcs and rotate as native scroll progress advances through the harbor. Scrolling up reverses those transformations. Reduced motion hides the moving birds and paper repairs together to reveal the original still engraving.

Issue #26 groups the original harbor, sampled water extension and passing wave under one co-moving ink parent. The harbor no longer receives an additional scroll translation. The wave’s visible hatching comes from the sampled archival water; its curved clipping edge is a modern adaptation. The independent original gull motion remains inside that group.
