# Proposed asset refinement: authentic whaling logbook on candlelit desk

**Status:** Creative direction proposal, not an approved automatic merge.  
**Scene:** Opening candlelit desk; place beside, not inside, the modern historical-statistics manuscript.

## Historical asset

**Title:** *Drawings of Whales in the Log of the Ship Indian Chief*  
**Keeper:** Thomas R. Bloomfield  
**Date:** 1842–1844  
**Source:** Nantucket Historical Association / Internet Archive, via Public Domain Image Archive  
**Original and rights:** https://pdimagearchive.org/images/6d2ba1f4-e8ef-4085-8090-0cd89135051a/  
**Image size:** 5072 × 4040; listed as public domain worldwide, no additional digital rights.

Related editorial reading: https://publicdomainreview.org/essay/the-art-of-whaling/

The open spread juxtaposes intricate blue-toned drawings of whales on one page with handwritten entries on the facing page. This is a **real whaling document from the period before the novel's 1851 publication**. Do not redraw its marks or substitute invented text. Do not assert that these pages document the Pequod or give the logbook an invented provenance.

## Proposed staging and learning interaction

- Place the **open, intact logbook** at an angle on the timber desk, alongside the existing economic-statistics editorial sheet.
- Let the spermaceti candle's scroll-linked illumination reveal the editorial sheet first and sweep over the colored whale drawings and cursive logbook page second.
- Retain the logbook's original pale paper, blue ink/drawn whales, and handwriting; avoid a fake modern overlay printed *onto* the archival page.
- Give it spatial realism (perspective, contact shadow, restrained texture and depth) but keep the scan sharp enough that it is recognizable as a real object. Preserve desk/candle layout and legibility.
- On touch/keyboard interaction, offer a **zoomable detail or readable modal** with a short note: nineteenth-century officers used illustrated logbooks to document sightings, catches and voyages. Link back to the original artifact and The Public Domain Review analysis. Do not claim that every blue whale mark is a kill without consulting the actual entries; there were distinctions between sightings and catches.
- Avoid making the logbook a fake source for the modern 1846 fleet and salary figures. Explicitly distinguish the historical **primary-source logbook** from the site's **modern annotations**.
- On a phone, do not crowd the manuscript text. Consider showing part of the logbook at first, with tap-to-expand, or alternating which paper occupies the illuminated area.
- Use a responsibly optimized derivative (WebP/AVIF), plus source/credit metadata; do not deploy the full 5072×4040 scan as an uncompressed hero asset.

## Why this matters

This artifact creates a direct connection between sailors' recordkeeping, the economic accounting of whale hunting, and the novel's interest in reducing the immeasurable sea to charts, classifications and figures. Its aesthetic is richer because it is genuinely contemporary to the whaling industry, rather than invented period-style decoration.

## Acceptance

1. The whale drawings and authentic handwriting are readable on inspection.
2. The document behaves like a physical book *on* the desk, under the candlelight, not a floating UI panel.
3. The historical artifact and modern economic notes remain distinct and correctly sourced.
4. No mobile text occlusion; reduced-motion and keyboard access remain functional.
5. Performance stays smooth.
