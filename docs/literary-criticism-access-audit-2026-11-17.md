# Eight priority Melville essays — checked access routes (2026-11-17)

**Scope:** Source discovery and manual-download leads only. Not a claim that the Melville scholar has read full books, and not an invitation to commit copyrighted PDFs to this public repository.

**Critical finding:** The user can download **one verified full author-hosted Cambridge essay** directly (Hester Blum). Geoffrey Sanborn lists his 2013 Cambridge essay as a downloadable upload on his own Academia.edu faculty page, but **the platform blocked automated retrieval**: sign-in and PDF completeness have not been verified. An official Cambridge preview supplies only the introduction and the very first page of Sanborn's chapter. No accessible complete copy was confirmed for the other six original targeted essays.

## Additional HIGH-VALUE accessible sources

**Geoffrey Sanborn, “Moby-Dick” (2022)** from *A New Companion to Herman Melville*. Author-posted public text: https://www.academia.edu/101864817/_Moby_Dick_ . **Not identical to his 2013 Cambridge essay.** The published essay's text is publicly inspectable in the author-posted page. Particularly useful for the site's purpose: immersion, non-semantic rhythms and alliteration, neglected chapters and associative movement, and especially Ch. 57's progression from carved whale images into rocks and constellations. This may be more directly useful to the founder's interaction concept than the 2013 chapter. Do not upload reproduced full text to public GitHub.

**John Bryant, “Textual Identity and Adaptive Revision”** or related chapter (an independently authored argument, not “Moby-Dick as Revolution”), hosted at Melville Electronic Library: https://melville.electroniclibrary.org/pdf/john_bryant_essay_ch3.pdf . It cites “Moby-Dick as Revolution” but is NOT that essay; never conflate them.

**Official Cambridge preview** to *The New Cambridge Companion*: https://assets.cambridge.org/97811076/87912/excerpt/9781107687912_excerpt.pdf . The editor's introduction outlines several contributors' claims and then begins the Sanborn chapter. Label as **summary/partial** when used.

## Fastest user action

1. Download Hester Blum's full PDF direct from the author.
2. Open Sanborn's original 2013 Academia entry; attempt the author-provided full PDF (may require account), and save his more relevant 2022 “Moby-Dick” essay if access permits.
3. For **Franchot/Bryant 1998**, obtain the 1998 *Cambridge Companion to Herman Melville* through university/public library.
4. For **Otter 2013**, request from ResearchGate or obtain the 2013 *New Cambridge Companion to Herman Melville* through library.
5. For **three Norton pieces**, get **one** 2018 Norton 3rd-edition copy, ISBN **9780393285000**, and select target pages 663 onwards and 686–692.
6. If the founder supplies copies for private research, record in a permissioned work area and keep only original notes and bibliographic references here.

**Compliance:** These scholarly texts remain copyrighted even when author-hosted for free. Do not bundle/repost them as site assets or commit them in this public repo. Cite exact editions and pages as verified.
