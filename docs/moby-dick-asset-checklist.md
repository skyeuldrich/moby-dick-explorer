# Moby-Dick Historical Asset Checklist

This checklist defines the visual asset pass for the current prototype. The aim is to replace placeholder/generic imagery with historically grounded material while keeping the experience atmospheric and coherent.

## 1. Abyss opening / deep-sea darkness

### Goal
Make the opening read as the ocean abyss rather than generic blackness.

### Needed assets
- Deep-ocean background texture or gradient
- Extremely faint bioluminescent organisms
- A few identifiable deep-sea forms, such as:
  - anglerfish
  - jellyfish or siphonophore-like forms
  - tiny drifting luminous organisms
- Subtle drifting-motion treatment

### Visual requirements
- Creatures should register first as tiny moving stars
- On closer inspection, their forms should become faintly recognizable as sea life
- Keep the effect atmospheric, not cute or flashy
- Avoid bright neon-blue sci-fi treatment

### Likely asset type
Mostly custom-designed or generated visual assets, grounded in real deep-sea references.

### Research references needed
- Deep-sea photography
- Scientific illustrations
- Bioluminescence references

### Deliverables
- 1 background layer
- 3–6 faint creature sprites or motifs
- motion behavior notes

---

## 2. Emergence-of-light transition

### Goal
As the user scrolls, a faint warm light should emerge from below the abyss and resolve into the candle scene.

### Needed assets
- Soft amber glow transition
- Dark-to-warm color progression
- Optional haze or subtle particles

### Visual requirements
- The light should feel distant at first
- Transition should be smooth and scroll-linked
- Avoid an abrupt reveal

### Likely asset type
Designed transition rather than archival imagery.

### Deliverables
- transition spec
- optional glow texture asset

---

## 3. Spermaceti candle scene

### Goal
Make the candle look like a historically plausible spermaceti candle rather than a generic candle.

### Needed assets
- Spermaceti candle visual
- Period-appropriate candle holder
- Desk or tabletop setting
- Flame and glow treatment
- Accurate wax texture

### Visual requirements
- Smooth ivory or creamy wax
- Plain, elegant 19th-century feel
- Warm, luminous, intimate
- Avoid fantasy-candle or stock-photo aesthetics

### Likely asset type
Reference-grounded rendering or composite, supported by archival references where useful.

### Research references needed
- Historical spermaceti candles
- 19th-century candlesticks and interiors

### Deliverables
- final candle hero image
- optional responsive crop

---

## 4. Manuscript / whaling facts panel

### Goal
Present whaling-economy facts on a surface that feels like a real period manuscript, ledger, or printed sheet.

### Needed assets
- Old paper texture
- Manuscript or ledger layout
- Historically appropriate typography or handwriting
- Restrained headings and annotations

### Visual requirements
- Must remain readable
- Archival feel without excessive visual noise
- Avoid fake pirate-map aesthetics

### Likely asset type
Designed asset grounded in historical references rather than a literal archival page.

### Research references needed
- 19th-century shipping ledgers
- whaling logs
- account books
- broadsides and manuscripts

### Deliverables
- manuscript panel background
- text style guide
- layout rules for facts

---

## 5. Antique voyage map

### Goal
Use a real-looking historical maritime chart, then animate the Pequod's route over it.

### Needed assets
- Antique nautical map or chart
- Route-line overlay
- Relevant place names or ocean labels where useful
- Optional paper-texture cleanup

### Visual requirements
- Authentic rather than decorative fake-vintage
- Route line must remain legible
- Scroll progression should clearly advance the voyage

### Likely asset type
Prefer a public-domain archival map plus a modern route overlay.

### Research references needed
- 19th-century nautical charts
- world/ocean maps appropriate to whaling routes

### Preferred source pools
- Library of Congress
- NYPL Digital Collections
- Internet Archive
- Wikimedia Commons
- maritime archives

### Deliverables
- selected map candidate
- cleaned/cropped asset
- route-overlay specification

---

## 6. Whale anatomical illustration

### Goal
Use a legitimate historical cetology or whale-anatomy image instead of generic whale art.

### Needed assets
- Whale anatomical plate or engraved illustration
- Optional labels/details
- Optional zoom and crop variants

### Visual requirements
- Real 18th- or 19th-century natural-history feel
- Detailed, serious, scholarly
- Prefer engraved or lithographic linework
- Should feel like entering a cetological archive

### Likely asset type
Prefer a public-domain archival image.

### Research references needed
- 19th-century cetology books
- whale anatomy plates
- natural-history engravings
- whaling manuals

### Preferred source pools
- Biodiversity Heritage Library
- Internet Archive
- Wikimedia Commons
- museum and university collections

### Deliverables
- 2–4 candidate plates
- one selected final asset
- desktop/mobile crop guidance

---

## 7. Descent back into the abyss

### Goal
After the whale illustration, transition downward into oceanic darkness again.

### Needed assets
- Return-to-abyss background
- Darkening transition
- Optional reappearance of faint bioluminescent life

### Visual requirements
- Should feel like a return to the deep, not a generic fade to black
- Echo the opening visually
- Can become more ominous than the opening

### Likely asset type
Designed transition/background.

### Deliverables
- end-state background
- transition behavior notes

---

# Cross-section authenticity rules

## Visual authenticity
- Prefer public-domain archival imagery where possible
- Use generated/new visuals only when:
  - no suitable archival option exists
  - motion or continuity requires it
  - compositing is necessary
- Avoid:
  - stock-photo feel
  - generic AI historical-fantasy imagery
  - cartoonish ocean imagery
  - fake parchment clichés

## Style cohesion
All sections should feel like parts of one work:
- dark
- serious
- immersive
- archival/museum quality
- restrained typography
- no abrupt stylistic jumps
