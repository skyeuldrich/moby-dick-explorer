The founder requested that the moving map follow the novel’s voyage from Nantucket. Two independent, read-only Melville Scholar passes read the complete text of chapters 1–34 and 35–68, including the digressions. Together these exceed half the book’s word count. The lead independently checked the decisive route passages and reconciled their findings. This replaces the previous generic Pacific leg; it does not change the novel to fit an available map.

| 1 | Nantucket harbor → open Atlantic | [22, “Merry Christmas”](https://www.gutenberg.org/files/2701/2701-h/2701-h.htm#link2HCH0022): “It was a short, cold Christmas”; pilots leave after the ship gains an offing; “blindly plunged like fate into the lone Atlantic.” | Actual departure. **Exact order of named grounds is possible; an exact plotted course is not.** The novel is not a surviving ship’s navigational log. Connecting curves, distances offshore, and chart-space points must be presented as reconstructions. The animation’s speed is narrative pacing, not elapsed voyage time.

## Important exclusions

- Chapters 1–13 follow Ishmael’s arrival in New Bedford; chapter 13’s New Bedford → Nantucket passage is aboard the packet *Moss*. It is not the Pequod’s departure route.
- Chapters 14–21 concern Nantucket, recruitment, preparations, warnings, and intentions. Early references to Cape Horn are not proof of an achieved passage.
- Chapter 23’s lee-shore analogy supplies no named actual coast. Chapters 24–27 discuss general whaling history, crew origins, and custom. Chapter 27’s statement that Nantucket whalers recruit in the Azores does not establish a Pequod port call.
- Chapter 29’s “bright Quito spring” compares climate. Quito is neither a visited landfall nor a numerical tropical latitude fix. Do not insert a south-to-the-tropics-and-back-north detour before the Azores solely from the order of these passages.
- Chapters 30–34 add shipboard action, cetology, command structure, and the cabin table. Ahab calculates a latitude in 34; the result is withheld.
- Chapter 36’s threat to chase round Good Hope, Cape Horn, and the Norway Maelstrom is a vow, not an itinerary. Chapter 41’s Patagonian Cape concerns Ahab’s previous voyage after losing his leg.
- Chapter 44’s Cape Horn example is a hypothetical scheduling argument about the Season-on-the-Line. It does not override the actual Cape of Good Hope passage in 51.
- Chapter 45’s *Union* off the Azores is another vessel’s historical case. Chapter 54’s Lima frame and Pacific adventures belong to the *Town-Ho* story.
- Chapter 58’s Brazil Banks footnote is an explanation of brit. Chapter 61 compares the current Indian Ocean waters to the livelier Río de la Plata and Peru grounds; it does not place the Pequod off Peru then.
- Fleece’s Cape Town church memory in chapter 64 supplies no Pequod port call. Morocco and Gibraltar are not narrated present-voyage waypoints in this reading.

## What this prototype plots

**Nantucket → southward Atlantic passage → off the Azores → off Cape Verde.** The 1797 William Heather sea chart actually includes these regions. The route starts immediately seaward of the small printed Nantucket island, follows the explicit southward departure, crosses the North Atlantic toward the Azores, then turns south toward Cape Verde. The exact connecting shape is interpretive, not a fictional log passed off as evidence. Markers do not announce a future ground before the line reaches it; previous marker labels recede as the next leg advances.

This is deliberately the beginning requested by the founder. The chart stops at the Cape Verde cruising ground. The later Plate → Carrol → Good Hope → Crozets → toward Java sequence is documented in the page’s nonanimated reading alternative and field notes. It is not falsely drawn on a North Atlantic sheet that cannot show it. The anatomy and abyss remain thematic scenes, not the next literal position of the ship or a claim that the novel’s voyage ends at Cape Verde. Extending the plotted voyage would require additional southern Atlantic / Indian Ocean charts.

The artifact’s existing printed tracks are not the Pequod’s. The 1797 chart date is not the fictional voyage year. No new port call, exact day count, harbor pilot trace, or scientific survey accuracy is asserted.
