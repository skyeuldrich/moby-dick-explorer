## Important discovery: authentic whaling-used South Atlantic charts survive

**E. & G.W. Blunt, *Chart of the South Atlantic Ocean*, 1834, New York, engraved by W. Hooker.** [David Rumsey item 4610.000](https://www.davidrumsey.com/luna/servlet/detail/RUMSEY~8~1~245910~5514640). This map spans the equator to 65° S; the catalogue says it was held with Blunt's North Atlantic and Norie's northern/southern Pacific sheets and **used on whaling voyages by an American captain in the 1840s and 1850s**, with tracks and whale sightings recorded. It includes insets for St Helena and Table Bay. This is **a research-strength historical link between navigational paper and working whaling practice**, not a reason to represent its actual pencil markings as Ahab's. Preserve the distinction between real historical annotations and the site's fictional Chapter 44 graphite layer.

A second, more straightforward, high-resolution source is an **1840 edition of Blunt's South Atlantic chart** from Bibliothèque nationale de France, Gallica ID **btv1b53098744v**, circulated as a [Wikimedia Commons file](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Chart_of_the_South_Atlantic_Ocean_from_the_Equator_to_65%C2%B0_South_Latitude._According_to_the_latest_surveys_and_Observations_-_btv1b53098744v.jpg). Listed resolution **8,168 × 11,857**, 1840, source BnF. Commons tags the historical work as public-domain US/France; verify exact file reuse and site attribution language before copying it into the public repo. **Suggested choice for Folio I southern section, subject to actual pixel/crop inspection**. The 1834 Rumsey copy may be a stronger research/discovery object, but confirm permission before embedding its digital scan.

**Compared candidate:** [Maury's *Pilot chart of the South Atlantic*, 1850, Library of Congress](https://www.loc.gov/item/2009575948/), also [Commons high-resolution file](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Pilot_chart_of_the_South_Atlantic_LOC_2009575948.jpg), **7,062 × 10,890** pixels. LoC says its map-division files can be reused unless an item Rights Advisory says otherwise. Superb contemporary evidence for wind/science and historically near the novel; **not my first-choice route background**, since the wind-rose grid dominates the design. Consider it as a separate, optional historical investigation into mariners' claims to read and calculate the ocean. Do **not** confuse the 1850 LoC sheet with a distinct 1853 two-sheet edition.

**Rights distinction:** The underlying eighteenth/nineteenth-century engraving and a particular modern high-resolution digital scan can have separate item-level reuse rules or institutional terms. Wikimedia's public-domain classification is evidence, not a substitute for recording the **specific source, file URL, item dates and credits**. Rumsey's [current policy](https://www.davidrumsey.com/about/copyright-and-permissions) permits images in digital publications (including profit/nonprofit) while stating that downloaded files are subject to its linked Creative Commons license. Confirm how CC BY-NC-SA 3.0 applies to the site's annotated/cropped uses before publishing. Never label these scans CC0 without an exact statement.

## Recommended composition / interactive treatment, NOT yet visually mocked up

**Folio I — the Atlantic chart becomes two real sheets.** Preserve Heather's existing Nantucket → Azores → Cape Verde. At the transition to the Plate Ground, a physical *second chart sheet* appears, with Blunt's southern Atlantic coastlines. The red route first approaches the river mouth *offshore*, then turns east into St Helena/Carrol's region and eventually the Cape. The turning west-then-east is the discovery; a separate “Only Melville's evidence” action removes interpretive joining curves.

**Folio II — a large ocean sheet and a passage inset.** Treat Indian Ocean NE sailing as a long traversable blank expanse rather than accelerating across. Switch to a detailed Sunda/Philippine folio for the narrow maritime passages. When the Ch. 87 words **“purposing to pass”** appear, an intended route is drawn in interrupted form, teaching the difference between a plan and a confirmed course. Ch. 109 locates the later narrative near Formosa/Bashee without supplying missing daily positions.

**Folio III — Japan then unresolved equatorial ocean.** Use a true *northern/western* Pacific chart; let the historical page contain the named region, then let a wide equatorial **uncertainty zone** remain in place of a terminal pin. The limit of chartable evidence becomes the transition into the already-built black abyss. Do not touch the three quotations, organisms, or whale/squid reveal.

For EACH source-backed viewport, prepare two actual crops **after image access**: desktop 1440×900, phone 390×844, showing title/year/archive plaque, unobscured printed chart, red hypothesis/route distinction, and text that remains legible. Reverse scroll and sheet seam must be tested. These are **production specifications, not claims of completed visual mockups**.
