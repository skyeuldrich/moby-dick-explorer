## Why this matters
What does Ahab claim to know about the force he wants to strike? The chapter
frames the confrontation theatrically, beginning with a stage direction, and
turns a workplace exchange into a public oath. A first reading may treat the
whale as simply the agent of evil; Ahab himself admits uncertainty about what
lies beyond the visible surface.

## Two readings grounded in the text
1. **Revolt against an apparent hidden power:** Ahab describes the whale as a
   wall and imagines a reasoning thing behind an unreasoning mask. His desire
   to strike through it is more than a commercial hunt. But “Sometimes I think
   there’s naught beyond” troubles any confident account of that power.
2. **Ahab's projection and coercion:** Starbuck calls the blow “blindest
   instinct”; Ahab nevertheless promises to strike whether the whale is
   “agent” or “principal.” The contrast exposes how his need for an adversary
   overrides a competing explanation. Yet Ahab's wounded body and his own
   uncertainty prevent the scene from being only a neat diagnosis.

## Educational experience
- **30 seconds:** place Starbuck's “blindest instinct” beside Ahab's “agent”
  or “principal” (roughly, an instrument of another force or acting on its
  own). Ask what Starbuck objects to and what Ahab remains unsure about. Make
  the surrounding exchange and full chapter one action away.
- **Alternative:** sequence the crew's response, the gold, and the oath to ask
  how a private grievance becomes a collective undertaking.
- **3 minutes:** follow the speech from mask to “naught beyond” to Starbuck's
  prayer, then compare how public pressure changes the conversation.
- Objective: distinguish a character's claim from the novel's verdict.
  Use keyboard-operable text choices and a no-motion reading path; do not make
  a choice imply that the whale's motives are settled.
- Check: can a visitor identify both Starbuck's objection and Ahab's
  uncertainty? This is a proposed test, not an observed outcome.
