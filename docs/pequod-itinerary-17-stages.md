# The Pequod's seventeen-stage itinerary — founder-supplied geographic handoff

**Status:** Founder supplied this itinerary after consulting the complete novel and Norton Critical Edition (third edition, 2018), on November 18, 2026. This table is the **approved editorial starting point** for a later global atlas, with the distinctions below. It is **not an exact nautical track** and does not license coding agents to invent coordinates or conduct another literary survey.

Source image supplied in chat: a scholarly **Norton world-map illustration of Melville's voyages and the Pequod**. That figure is a **private visual reference** for understanding the macro itinerary, not an image we can reproduce, trace as a map asset, or commit to the public repository without documented permission.

**What we know:** sea basins, grounds, named passages, and the equatorial Pacific endpoint. **What we do not know:** daily ship positions, exact water tracks between grounds, exact dates throughout the voyage, or the final longitude/wreck coordinates. A HIGH score for an anchor's being *named* is **not** a high score for the shape of the connecting line.

## Essential corrections to a misleading Atlantic-only map

The current website's red line ends off Cape Verde because it was written as a **prototype vertical slice**. **The novel does not end the Atlantic leg there.** In Chapter 51 Ishmael states that the ship had passed **Azores → Cape Verde → Plate → Carrol** grounds. The continuation across the South Atlantic is indispensable to the founder's requested reconstruction. It then heads east past the Cape and the Crozets, across the Indian Ocean, through Indonesian waters to the western Pacific, and turns southeast toward the equator.

Avoid an invented straight voyage from **Nantucket → Azores → Cape Verde → Cape of Good Hope → Japan**; the crossing toward the Río de la Plata and return toward St. Helena are essential features of the narration.

**Especially important distinction:** Ch. 87 describes a **route Ahab “purposing to pass”** through Sunda/Javan waters toward the Philippines and Japan; Chs. 109–111 provide later position evidence. A thick, exact, unbroken line through each archipelago would overclaim the source. Use **thin or interrupted conjectural links**, marked “inferred / planned,” when evidence does not fix an exact path.

## Rendering data model — explicit distinctions

For each stage codify **id, index, stage-label, chapter link(s), confidence, location type (named point / ocean zone / direction / inferred bridge), geographic display scope, caption and evidence type**. If a segment has no geographic endpoints, **do not make up latitude/longitude**. For broad grounds use zones, not highly precise pins; only named islands/capes may be grounded to approximate coordinates **with an explicit note that the ship's exact position is unknown**.

Prefer a few well-designed **folding-chart transitions** rather than 17 popups. Each stop can expose a source passage, simple date/confidence label and why it matters; the actual route line is a visual reading tool, not an empirical reconstruction.

Three atlases/sheets are an appealing structure:
1. Atlantic folio — Heather 1797 archive (exists; most of stages 1–7).
2. Indian Ocean + Southeast Asian archipelago folio — **new exact archival asset(s) required**, source verification and founder approval.
3. Pacific folio — Norie 1844 historical source already in repo may cover a portion; **verify geographic coverage and orientation** before promising Japanese grounds or the equatorial endpoint. Additional appropriate sheet may be needed.

Any later global overview must be dated to its **own** edition, not misleadingly labeled an 1840s shipboard chart. The Norton map remains an offsite editorial reference, not an asset.
